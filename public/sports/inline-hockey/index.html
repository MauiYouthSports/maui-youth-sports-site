Maui Youth Inline Hockey

Last checked Nov 2026

On Maui, youth inline hockey has one home: the outdoor rink at Kalama Park in Kīhei, run by the nonprofit Maui Inline Hockey Association (MIHA). Youth hockey is on the rink Monday through Thursday at 5 p.m., and families register through MIHA's own website. Here's what families need to know to get a keiki rolling.

What inline hockey looks like for keiki

Inline hockey is hockey on wheels. Players wear inline skates instead of ice skates and play on a hard outdoor surface, so it fits our weather all year. Kids learn skating, stickhandling, passing, shooting and how to play as a team. Because the game never really stops, balance and skating confidence grow fast, and those skills carry over to other sports too.

MIHA describes its program as alcohol, tobacco and drug free, and it's built on volunteers: coaches, referees, scorekeepers and parents who help keep the rink going.

Ages and age groups

MIHA builds its youth teams by age group each season, based on how many players sign up. Ask MIHA which divisions are running this season and where your child fits.

How to sign up: two steps

Registration for the 2026–27 year opened Aug. 15, 2026. Every player needs both steps, and MIHA recommends doing them on a computer or tablet.

  1. AAU membership first. MIHA is an AAU-sanctioned league, so every player, coach and referee needs a current AAU membership. AAU switched to a new system for 2026–27, so last year's login may not work. Enter MIHA's club code (listed on MIHA's registration page), use the same name you'll use with MIHA, and write down your AAU membership number.
  2. MIHA league registration. Register online with your AAU number. You'll fill in player and guardian info, choose a registration option, and sign the HEADS UP concussion waiver and the code of conduct. MIHA notes that players who don't register won't be placed on a team.

Stuck? Email mauiinlinehockeyassociation@gmail.com or leave a message at 808-874-5655.

Registration details and help

Registration options, forms, deadlines and current rates are on MIHA's own registration page, so check there first. The youth year runs September through August: two seasons, then summer practice.

If money is tight, don't let that stop you. MIHA says it can help players who may not have the means to play. Email the association or talk with Coach Ed at the rink. Scholarships and assistance are listed separately.

Gear and getting ready

Before you buy anything, ask MIHA for its current youth gear list and whether loaner gear is available for new players. Secondhand gear is common in hockey, so ask other rink families too.

If your child is new to skating, public skate is a low-pressure way to build confidence first. It runs Friday and Saturday from 5 to 9 p.m. Rental skates are available at the rink, and MIHA's public skate page has the details. Public skate is general skating, not a hockey class, and it's run entirely by volunteers.

Season at a glance

For 2026–27, the youth league began Aug. 18 and games started Sept. 17. The youth year runs two seasons, then summer practice. Check MIHA's season page for the next registration window.

Parent FAQ

What age can my child start inline hockey on Maui?

MIHA forms youth teams by age group each season. Contact MIHA for the current divisions and the youngest age they take.

Does my child need to know how to skate first?

Skating is the foundation of the game. Ask MIHA how it supports beginners, and use Friday and Saturday public skate to practice.

Why do we need an AAU membership?

MIHA is an AAU-sanctioned league, and every player has to complete AAU membership before MIHA registration will go through.

When does the season start?

For 2026–27, registration opened Aug. 15, the youth league began Aug. 18, and games started Sept. 17. Watch MIHA's season page for the next registration window.

Can my child join partway through the year?

Ask MIHA. Registration runs through the league, and players must be registered to be placed on a team.

Is there financial help?

Yes. MIHA says it can assist players who may not have the means to play. Email mauiinlinehockeyassociation@gmail.com or talk with Coach Ed at the rink. See also scholarships and assistance.

Can parents help out?

MIHA is always looking for volunteers, including public skate helpers, coaches, referees, rink cleanup and scorekeepers.
