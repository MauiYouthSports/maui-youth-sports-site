Maui Youth Skateboarding

Last checked Nov 2026

Maui has a skatepark in nearly every corner of the island, from Kīhei to Hāna, plus one on Molokaʻi. Most are County parks open every day. For coached help, keiki can join One Love Skate's weekly youth groups in Pukalani and Pāʻia, or come to a community skate jam that starts with a beginner lesson. Here's where to skate and how to get started. Love wheels? Try inline hockey at Kalama Park.

Why skateboarding is great for keiki

Skateboarding is all about trying, falling and getting back up. Kids set their own goals, like a first drop-in or a first ollie, and progress at their own pace. Skateparks are also real community spaces, where older skaters often cheer on the little ones. A coached group or a skate jam is a great way for a new skater to start out on the right foot. Read our parents' guide to concussions before a first session.

A few highlights for parents:

  • Keōpūolani has a street course with beginner-friendly areas, plus a big 11-foot vert ramp for advanced riders.
  • Kalama sits steps from the beach in Kīhei, with a street course, a small bowl and a vert ramp connected to a mini ramp.
  • On Molokaʻi, the County notes that "Duke" Maliu's smaller wooden ramps are especially good for beginners and younger skaters.
  • StoneWave, next to Pāʻia Bay at the Pāʻia Youth & Cultural Center (PYCC), has bowls, a street area and a mini-ramp. It's open Tuesday through Saturday afternoons, and skate pass details are on PYCC's website. Helmets and shoes are required, and skaters under 18 need a waiver signed in front of PYCC staff. PYCC youth membership for ages 9–19 includes the skatepark waiver.

The County parks are public recreation spaces with no coach on duty, so younger skaters should go with a grown-up.

Coached groups and lessons

One Love Skate — weekly "Acts 4:20" groups

One Love Skate is a Maui nonprofit whose weekly groups mix coaching, mentoring and faith ("Practice, Prayer & Pizza"). Skaters work on four core skills at their own pace: drop-ins, manuals, ollies and powerslides. The current season began Sept. 3, 2026. Its Maui groups meet at:

  • Pukalani Skatepark: Thursdays, 4:30–5:30 p.m. (prayer at 4:20)
  • Pāʻia Youth & Cultural Center: contact One Love for the time

One Love asks families to confirm with the coach before coming, and group details are on its website.

Mana Mentors — West Maui

A West Maui youth mentoring nonprofit that partners with One Love on skate contests and community events. Contact them for upcoming dates.

Free skate jams

One Love Skate and the County of Maui Department of Parks and Recreation host community skate jams around the island. Each one usually opens with a beginner lesson for newer skaters, then contests for different skill levels.

Next up: Pukalani Skate Jam, Saturday, Nov. 28, 2026, at Pukalani Skatepark. Registration opens at 2:45 p.m., a beginner lesson runs 3–3:30 p.m., and the jam goes until 6 p.m. Expect skill-level contests, 45-second runs, Best Trick, food, games and prizes. Helmets are required, pads are recommended, a limited amount of equipment is available to borrow, and every skater needs a completed waiver. See all upcoming events.

Gear and safety

  • Helmet, every session. It's required at StoneWave and at the skate jams, and it's smart everywhere.
  • Pads (knee, elbow, wrist) are a good idea for beginners.
  • Flat-soled shoes. No slippers.
  • Water and shade breaks. Maui afternoons get hot. See our heat safety guide.

Parent FAQ

Which Maui skateparks are good for beginners?

Keōpūolani has beginner-friendly areas, and on Molokaʻi "Duke" Maliu is especially well suited to new and younger skaters. The beginner lesson at a skate jam is also a great first step.

Are helmets required?

Helmets are required at StoneWave and at the One Love/County skate jams. Even where they aren't required, keiki should wear one.

Are there skateboard lessons for kids on Maui?

Yes. One Love Skate runs weekly coached groups in Pukalani and Pāʻia, and the community skate jams start with a beginner lesson.

Do kids need a grown-up at the skatepark?

The County parks are public spaces with no coach on duty, so younger keiki should skate with a parent or trusted adult. StoneWave is part of the Pāʻia Youth & Cultural Center and has its own waiver and rules.

My child doesn't have a board yet. Can they still try?

At skate jams, a limited amount of equipment is available to borrow. For everyday skating, ask a local skate shop to help size a first board.
