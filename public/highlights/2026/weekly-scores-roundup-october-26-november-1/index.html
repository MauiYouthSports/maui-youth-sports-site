Weekly scores roundup, Oct. 26–Nov. 1: Seabury Hall wins a state title, Lahainaluna edges Maui High

Published 2026-11-01

Seabury Hall finished 19–0 with its first HHSAA Division II girls volleyball state championship, four Maui teams played at the state tournament, and Lahainaluna held off Maui High 13–10 in football.

State tournament week took Maui’s volleyball teams to Oʻahu, and one of them came home with the trophy. Here’s every final score we confirmed from Monday, Oct. 26, through Sunday, Nov. 1, plus two league playoff results we’ve now been able to add.

Girls volleyball: Seabury Hall are state champions

Seabury Hall 3, Waipahu 1 (Friday, Oct. 30, HHSAA Division II championship at the Stan Sheriff Center). The top-seeded Spartans won 25–18, 25–18, 12–25, 25–18 to finish 19–0 and claim the program’s first Division II state title, based on the HHSAA bracket and record book. Milaniakai Padilla led with 21 kills and 12 digs, Dillon McLellan added 16 kills, Kamalei Stewart had 10 kills, D. Keomaka-Wood set up 39 assists and Tulsi Erlemann had 25 digs, per the HHSAA box score. It was Seabury’s 14th straight win, according to MaxPreps. Read the championship story.

To get there, Seabury Hall beat Kaiser 3–1 (23–25, 25–20, 25–19, 28–26) in Wednesday’s quarterfinal, with Padilla putting up 28 kills and 21 digs (HHSAA box score), then swept Le Jardin 25–17, 25–23, 25–14 in Thursday’s semifinal, when Padilla had 14 kills and six aces (HHSAA box score). Read the semifinal story.

Hāna opened the Division II tournament against Kaʻū on Tuesday at Kalani and took the first set 25–21 before Kaʻū won the next three, 25–21, 27–25, 25–8. Java Cantero had 11 kills and three aces and Destiny Perry had 22 assists for Hāna (HHSAA box score). Hāna’s run ended Wednesday with a 25–15, 25–16 consolation loss to Hawaiʻi Prep, per the HHSAA bracket. Read our Hāna story.

Kamehameha Maui won the first set 25–18 against Nānākuli in Monday’s Division I first round at home, then dropped three close ones, 19–25, 24–26, 23–25. Emmali Brash had 17 kills, 17 digs and five aces, Avei Hiona Leauanae added 17 kills and Ciara Poepoe had 43 assists (HHSAA box score). Read the story.

King Kekaulike, the MIL Division I champion, fell 25–19, 25–11, 25–18 to Moanalua in Wednesday’s quarterfinal at Kamehameha Kapālama. Nanea Guarin had seven kills and Chiyomi Sales had 13 digs (HHSAA box score). Nā Aliʻi’s season ended Thursday with a 25–16, 25–13 consolation loss to Mililani, per the HHSAA bracket.

Football

Lahainaluna 13, Maui High 10 (Friday, Oct. 30, at Lahainaluna). The Lunas held on for their third win in four home games and moved to 3–5, while the Sabers fell to 3–6 after a third straight loss, according to MaxPreps. Lahainaluna’s home defense has allowed 14 points a game over that stretch (MaxPreps). Player stats haven’t been posted yet.

Kūlanihākoʻi was scheduled to play at King Kekaulike on Saturday, Oct. 31. We’re still confirming that final and will add it once it checks out.

Up next: King Kekaulike visits Kamehameha Maui on Friday, Nov. 6, at 7 p.m., and Baldwin and Lahainaluna close the regular season on Saturday, Nov. 7, at 7 p.m., per the MIL schedule. Keep up with every game in our MIL Football Schedule 2026 guide.

Catching up: MIL Division II volleyball playoffs

Two results from the MIL Division II girls volleyball playoffs the week before are now confirmed with Hāna’s athletics department. Hāna 3, Molokaʻi 1 (Wednesday, Oct. 21; 21–25, 25–21, 25–11, 25–15) and Hāna 3, Lānaʻi 1 (Thursday, Oct. 22; 22–25, 25–23, 25–18, 25–16). The Lānaʻi matchup is also listed on MaxPreps.

What a week for Maui volleyball. Congratulations to the Spartans, and mahalo to every player, coach and family who represented Maui County at states. Have a score we missed? Send it to us.
