Kamehameha Maui beats unbeaten Kalaheo 29–11, returns to the Division II state final

Published 2026-11-21

The No. 1 Warriors forced three interceptions, returned a fumble for a touchdown and ran for more than 200 yards. They play for a third straight state title on Saturday, Nov. 28, at Mililani.

Kamehameha Maui is headed back to the state championship game. The top-seeded Warriors beat No. 4 Kalaheo 29–11 in a Hawaii High School Athletic Association Division II semifinal on Friday night, Nov. 20, at Kanaʻiaupuni Stadium in Pukalani, according to the HHSAA Division II bracket. It was the first loss of the season for Kalaheo, the Oʻahu Interscholastic Association champion from Kailua, which came to Maui at 11–0.

The win sends the two-time defending champions to the Division II final on Saturday, Nov. 28, at 4 p.m. at John Kauinana Stadium at Mililani High School. KHON2 reported it is the Warriors’ fourth straight trip to the Division II title game.

How the game turned

Kalaheo struck first. Loch Moorman kicked a 28-yard field goal, and the Mustangs led 3–0 after one quarter, according to the official HHSAA game statistics.

Kamehameha Maui took over in the second quarter with 14 straight points. Quarterback Ryder De Silva ran 6 yards for a touchdown, the Warriors’ defense added a safety, and Kaikea Hueu scooped up a Kalaheo fumble and returned it 21 yards for a score. Both conversion tries after the touchdowns failed, and the Warriors led 14–3 at halftime.

Xander Pagan broke a 24-yard touchdown run in the third quarter, and Eli Caires added the kick for a 21–3 lead. Hueu scored again in the fourth quarter on a 6-yard run, and De Silva passed to Cyrus Andrade for the 2-point conversion to make it 29–3.

Kalaheo’s only touchdown came after that: Moorman threw an 11-yard scoring pass to Storm Kane and then hit Jayden Carreiro for the 2-point conversion.

Key stats

  • Running game: Pagan carried 21 times for 95 yards and a touchdown. Duke Beltran added 66 yards on five carries and Kale Peauope 33 yards on six. The Warriors finished with 206 rushing yards on the official stat sheet.
  • Passing: De Silva completed 4 of 15 passes for 42 yards with one interception. Kamalei Bullock caught two passes for 35 yards.
  • Takeaways: Kamakana Casco, Urijah Benz-Ah Yen and Teyvon Kahalewai each intercepted a pass.
  • Pressure: Kapena Lani led the defense with 8.5 tackles and 2.5 sacks, Nakoa Pau had two sacks, and the Warriors were credited with seven sacks as a team. Elai Mossman had a sack and a fumble recovery, and Hueu had 4.5 tackles to go with his two touchdowns.
  • Holding the Mustangs down: Kalaheo running back Marquez Mellor, who came in with 1,475 rushing yards and 23 touchdowns according to the Star-Advertiser, was held to 42 yards on 11 carries. Kalaheo finished with 30 net rushing yards. Moorman completed 13 of 27 passes for 129 yards, and Kane caught eight passes for 74 yards.

Next up: a familiar foe at Mililani

Kamehameha Maui’s opponent in the final comes from the other semifinal, Kaiser vs. No. 2 Waimea on Saturday, Nov. 21, at 2:30 p.m. at Hanapēpē Stadium on Kauaʻi, according to the HHSAA bracket and the Star-Advertiser scoreboard. Either way, it’s a rematch of a recent title game. The Warriors beat Kaiser 37–14 for their first state championship in 2024 and beat Waimea 48–24 in last year’s final. Kaiser reached the semifinals with a 22–20 first-round win at No. 3 Honokaʻa. Read our preview of the Warriors’ title run.

Also at Mililani on Nov. 28, No. 1 Waipahu, a 42–0 semifinal winner over Saint Damien on Friday, plays the Kapaʻa–Kamehameha-Hawaiʻi winner for the Division I title at 7:30 p.m., according to the HHSAA Division I bracket. No. 1 Kapolei and No. 2 Kamehameha meet for the Open Division title on Saturday, Nov. 21, at 6 p.m. at the Clarence T.C. Ching Athletics Complex, per the Open Division bracket.

Also on the court

Two MIL boys basketball teams opened their seasons on Thursday, Nov. 19, in a preseason tournament at the Seabury Hall gym in Makawao. Seabury Hall rolled past Island School 63–28 in its home opener, and Lahainaluna beat ʻAiea 66–55 to start 1–0, according to MaxPreps (Seabury Hall recap, Lahainaluna recap).
