Kamehameha Maui vs. Waimea: what to know before the Division II state final

Published 2026-11-23

The schools that won the last four Division II football titles meet again Saturday, Nov. 28, at 4 p.m. at Mililani. Here’s how Waimea got there, the history between the teams and how to watch.

For the third time in four years, the Division II state football championship comes down to Kamehameha Maui and Waimea. The No. 1 Warriors (8–2) and the No. 2 Menehune from Kauaʻi kick off Saturday, Nov. 28, at 4 p.m. at John Kauinana Stadium at Mililani High School, according to the HHSAA Division II bracket.

Between them, these two programs have won every Division II title since 2022, The Maui News noted. Waimea won in 2022 and 2023. Kamehameha Maui won in 2024 and 2025, and now goes for a third straight.

How Waimea got back to Mililani

Waimea is the Kauaʻi Interscholastic Federation’s Division II team. The Menehune all but locked up that spot on Oct. 17 with an 18–7 come-from-behind win over Kauaʻi High in Kapaʻa, scoring all 18 points in the second half, The Garden Island reported.

Like the Warriors, Waimea had a first-round bye. In Saturday’s semifinal at Hanapēpē Stadium, the Menehune beat Kaiser, the Oʻahu Interscholastic Association runner-up, 36–26. They scored in every quarter and led 22–14 at halftime, according to the official HHSAA game statistics.

Waimea’s semifinal by the numbers

  • A big day on the ground: Paka Cintron-Chandler carried 21 times for 219 yards and scored on runs of 80, 12 and 7 yards. KHON2 called it a dominant performance.
  • A second workhorse: Kaikea Miyashiro added 148 yards on 26 carries and a 14-yard touchdown. Waimea ran for 373 yards as a team (our total of the official rushing lines) and threw only three passes.
  • Takeaways: Kepa Pablo intercepted two passes and Chance Gomes picked off another. Gomes also scored on a 1-yard run just before halftime.
  • Kicking: Wade Silva made a 22-yard field goal and three extra points.
  • What Kaiser did: Quarterback Koa Reis threw for 319 yards and three touchdowns, but Kaiser ran for just 17 yards (our total of the official rushing lines).

That sets up a clear matchup on Saturday. Waimea wants to run the ball, and the Warriors’ defense just held Kalaheo to 30 rushing yards with seven sacks in the semifinal. Read our story on Kamehameha Maui’s 29–11 win over Kalaheo.

A rivalry built in title games

  • 2022: Waimea 45, King Kekaulike 6
  • 2023: Waimea 31, Kamehameha Maui 28
  • 2024: Kamehameha Maui 37, Kaiser 14
  • 2025: Kamehameha Maui 48, Waimea 24

Those results come from the HHSAA tournament record book, which lists Kyle Linoz as Waimea’s coach for both of its titles and Ulima Afoa as Kamehameha Maui’s coach for both of its titles. In last year’s final, Xander Pagan scored on a 9-yard run and a 92-yard kickoff return, in the Warriors’ 48–24 win, the Hawaiʻi Journalism Initiative reported. Pagan is back this year and leads the Warriors in rushing, according to the Star-Advertiser.

A big day for Maui football

Kamehameha Maui’s win over Kalaheo means a Maui Interscholastic League team will play in the Division II final for the 10th straight state tournament, going back to Lahainaluna’s run of four straight titles from 2016 to 2019 (there was no tournament in 2020), according to the HHSAA record book. The MIL has won seven Division II titles: King Kekaulike in 2006, Lahainaluna from 2016 to 2019, and Kamehameha Maui in 2024 and 2025. A Warriors win would make it eight.

It’s a big night for Kauaʻi, too. After the Division II game, Kapaʻa plays No. 1 Waipahu for the Division I title at 7:30 p.m. on the same field, so both Kauaʻi schools will play for state titles at Mililani, The Garden Island reported.

How to watch or go

  • TV: Spectrum OC16 (channel 16, or 1016 in HD) is carrying the Division II final, Big Island Now reported.
  • Streaming: The game also streams on OC16 through the Spectrum News app and website, open to everyone with no login through Nov. 29, and live on YouTube at @spectrumlocalnews. It is also on the NFHS Network, which requires a subscription.
  • Tickets: The HHSAA sells state tournament tickets through its GoFan page.

Times and sites can change, so check with the HHSAA before you head out. For every MIL result this fall, see our MIL Football Schedule 2026 guide, and our earlier look at the Warriors’ title chase.
